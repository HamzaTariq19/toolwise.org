## When to use it

Use this tool to write posts for X (Twitter) that fit the 280-character limit, using the same counting rules as X itself. It also helps you split a long post into a thread, and shows how many words, hashtags, mentions and links you have used. It is handy for social media managers, writers drafting ahead, and anyone tired of trimming a post at the last moment.

## How to use it

1. Type or paste your post into **Your post**.
2. Watch the counter and ring. The ring turns orange when you have fewer than 20 characters left and red when you are over.
3. Check **Characters left**, **Words**, **Hashtags**, **Mentions** and **Links**.
4. If you go over the limit, **Split into a thread** appears with your post broken into parts. Tick **Add 1/3 numbers** to number them.
5. Click **Copy** to copy the post or each part of the thread.

Now paste a 600-character announcement. The counter turns red, and the thread splitter shows three parts, each under the limit and split between words. Tick **Add 1/3 numbers** and each part ends with 1/3, 2/3 or 3/3, so readers know there is more.

## Writing tighter posts

If you are a few characters over, look for easy cuts before splitting into a thread: replace "in order to" with "to", drop words like "really" and "very", and use numerals instead of words for numbers. These small edits often save 10 to 20 characters.

## Tips

- **Put the key point first.** Long posts are cut off with "Show more" on some screens.
- **Use one or two hashtags.** More looks cluttered and rarely helps reach.
- **Leave room for quotes.** If you want people to quote your post with a comment, a shorter post gives them space.
- **Read each thread part on its own.** Each one should make sense if someone sees it alone.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting letters yourself.** Links and emoji make simple counts wrong.
- **Splitting mid-sentence.** Edit the parts so each ends at a natural point.
- **Forgetting mentions use characters.** Every @name counts towards the limit.
