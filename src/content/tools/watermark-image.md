## When to use it

Watermark Image adds your name, website or logo on top of a photo. Photographers use it on previews and proofs, online sellers on product photos, artists on artwork they share online, and businesses on images posted to social media. A watermark shows who created the image and makes it less likely to be reused without credit.

## How to watermark an image

1. Tap **Choose image**, or drag a JPG, PNG, WebP or HEIC file onto the box.
2. Under **Watermark type**, choose **Text** (edit the words, such as "© Your Name") or **Logo image** (press **Choose logo**; PNG works best).
3. Pick a **Position** from the 3 × 3 grid, from top left to bottom right.
4. Tick **Repeat across the whole image** to cover the whole picture.
5. Adjust the **Size**, **Opacity** and, for text, the **Text color**.
6. Choose **Save as** and press **Download image**.

## A worked example

You're a wedding photographer sending a client 30 preview photos before final payment. For each preview, choose **Text**, type "© Ana Ruiz Photography – Preview", tick **Repeat across the whole image**, and set opacity to about 40%. The client can see every photo clearly but can't use them without the watermark. When they pay, you send the unwatermarked originals.

## Tips

- **Keep originals unwatermarked** and only watermark copies you share.
- **Use a consistent position and size** across a set of photos so they look professional.
- **Choose a colour that contrasts** with the area of the photo it sits on: white for dark areas, dark grey for light ones.
- **Make the watermark smaller** for social media posts and larger for proofs.
- **Add your website** so people who find the image know where it came from.

## Good watermark wording

- **© Name 2026** for portfolios and art.
- **yourshop.com** for product photos, so people can find you.
- **PROOF** or **PREVIEW** for client galleries before payment.

## Common mistakes

- **A huge, solid watermark** that ruins the photo for everyone, including potential clients.
- **Using a JPG logo,** which brings a white rectangle with it.
- **Placing it where a crop removes it,** such as a far corner of a wide photo shared as a square.
- **Watermarking low-resolution copies** that you might later need for print.

## Private by design

Your images are watermarked in your browser and never uploaded.
