## When to use it

The time zone converter shows what a particular date and time will be in other cities around the world. Use it to schedule an international meeting or call, plan a webinar or live stream for viewers in several countries, catch a flight connection, or check the right moment to message family abroad without waking them.

## How to convert a time

1. Enter the **Date** and **Time** you want to convert.
2. In **Time zone**, choose where that time applies, such as New York. Press **Now** to use the current moment.
3. The list shows the same moment in every city on your list, with its UTC offset, the date there, the time difference, and a label: working hours, evening/morning or night.
4. To add a place, type it in **Add a city or time zone** (for example Tokyo, Dubai or Los Angeles) and press **Add**. Remove a city with its ✕ button.

## A worked example

You're in New York and want to hold a video call at **9:00 AM on Wednesday 18 November 2026** with colleagues in London, Kolkata, Tokyo and Sydney. Enter that date and time with New York as the time zone. The converter shows London at **2:00 PM** (working hours), Kolkata at **7:30 PM**, Tokyo at **11:00 PM** and Sydney at **1:00 AM on Thursday** (night). Moving the call to 5:00 PM New York time gives Sydney 9:00 AM, but makes it 3:30 AM in Kolkata, so you may need two sessions.

## Tips for scheduling across time zones

- **Always include the time zone** in invitations, such as "9:00 AM New York (2:00 PM London)".
- **Watch the date line.** Asia and Australia are often already on the next day.
- **Check dates near March and October–November,** when clocks change in many countries.
- **Use UTC** for schedules involving many regions; it never changes for daylight saving.
- **Rotate awkward slots** in recurring meetings so the same team isn't always up late.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a fixed difference all year,** which breaks around daylight saving changes.
- **Using abbreviations like CST or IST,** which can mean different zones (IST is used for India, Ireland and Israel). Use city names instead.
- **Forgetting half-hour zones** such as India (UTC+5:30) and parts of Australia.
- **Converting from the wrong starting zone.** Check the Time zone field before sharing a time.
