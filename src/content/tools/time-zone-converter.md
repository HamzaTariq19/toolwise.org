## What this tool does

The time zone converter shows what a particular date and time in one place is in other cities around the world. It is perfect for scheduling international meetings, calls with family abroad, live events and travel. It accounts for daylight saving time automatically and highlights which cities are in normal working hours.

## How to use it

1. The current date, time and your own time zone are filled in. Press **Now** at any time to reset them.
2. Change the **date** and **time** to the moment you want to convert.
3. Change the **time zone** if the time you have is in another place. Start typing a city and pick from the list.
4. See the converted time in every city below. Use **Add** to include more cities and the cross button to remove one.

## Reading the results

Each card shows the local time, the date (which may be a day ahead or behind), the UTC offset and the difference from your starting time zone. A label tells you whether that time falls within working hours, the evening or morning, or the night.

## Tips for international scheduling

- **Always include the time zone** when sending a meeting time, for example "3 pm London time (UTC+0)".
- **Watch the date.** A Monday morning in Sydney is still Sunday afternoon in New York.
- **Daylight saving changes** happen on different dates in Europe, North America and the Southern Hemisphere, so the gap between cities can change for a few weeks in spring and autumn.
- **Some offsets are not whole hours.** India is UTC+5:30 and Nepal is UTC+5:45.
- **UTC** is a good neutral reference for technical work and international teams.
