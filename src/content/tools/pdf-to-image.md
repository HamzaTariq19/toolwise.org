## When to use it

PDF to Image turns PDF pages into JPG or PNG pictures. That's handy when you want to post a page on social media, put a slide or chart into a presentation or website, send a page through a messaging app that previews images but not PDFs, or upload a document to a form that only accepts images, such as an ID or certificate upload.

## How to convert PDF pages to images

1. Tap **Choose PDF file**, or drag a PDF onto the box.
2. Pick the **Image format**: **JPG** or **PNG**.
3. Choose the **Image quality**:
   - **Small (72 DPI), for screens**
   - **Normal (150 DPI)**
   - **High (300 DPI), for printing**
4. In **Pages**, leave it empty for every page, or type pages like `1-3, 5`.
5. Press **Convert to images**. Each page appears with a preview and its size in pixels.
6. Press **Download**. More than one image comes as a ZIP named pdf-images.zip.

Images are named after the PDF and page number, such as "brochure-page-3.jpg".

## A worked example

A government site wants your degree certificate as a JPG under 2 MB, but you have it as a 4-page PDF with the certificate on page 2. Choose **JPG**, keep **Normal (150 DPI)**, type `2` in **Pages** and press **Convert to images**. You get "certificate-page-2.jpg", around 1240 × 1754 pixels and well under 1 MB, sharp enough to read every word.

## What DPI means

DPI (dots per inch) sets how many pixels are made for each inch of the page. Higher DPI gives more detail and bigger files:

- **72 DPI:** fine for small previews; text looks soft when zoomed.
- **150 DPI:** clear on screens and good enough for most uploads.
- **300 DPI:** print quality; use for posters, flyers and fine print.

## Tips

- **Use PNG for charts, logos and text-heavy pages** so edges stay crisp.
- **Convert only the pages you need** to save time with long documents.
- **Crop afterwards** with Crop Image if you only want part of a page.
- **Need it smaller?** Run the images through Compress Image or Compress Image to KB.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing 300 DPI for web use.** The files are four times bigger than 150 DPI with no visible gain on screen.
- **Choosing PNG for photo-heavy brochures,** which can make each page several megabytes.
- **Expecting editable text.** For that, keep the original PDF.
- **Converting a locked PDF.** Unlock it first with its password.

## Private by design

Pages are rendered by your browser, so the PDF is never uploaded.
