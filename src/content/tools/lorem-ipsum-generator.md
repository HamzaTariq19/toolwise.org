## When to use it

The Lorem Ipsum generator creates placeholder text for designs, websites and documents before the real words are ready. Designers use it to test layouts and fonts, developers to fill templates and test how pages handle long or short content, and writers to sketch the structure of a brochure or presentation without getting stuck on wording.

## How to generate placeholder text

1. Enter **How many** you need.
2. Choose what they are **Of**: **Paragraphs**, **Sentences**, **Words** or **List items**.
3. Tick **Start with "Lorem ipsum…"** for the classic opening, and **Add HTML tags** if you're pasting into code.
4. Read the **Generated text**, then press **Copy text**.
5. Press **Generate new text** for a different mix.

## A worked example

You're designing a blog article template and want to see how it looks with a typical post. Ask for **5 paragraphs** with **Add HTML tags**, copy the result and paste it into your template's content area. Then generate **6 list items** to check bullet spacing, and **12 words** to test how a long headline wraps on a phone. Each piece shows a different part of the layout.

## Choosing the right amount

- **Headlines:** 6 to 12 words.
- **Card or product descriptions:** 1 to 3 sentences.
- **Blog posts:** 4 to 8 paragraphs.
- **Navigation and menus:** list items of 1 to 3 words.
- **Stress tests:** very long text, to check pages don't break.

## Tips

- **Test extremes:** try very short and very long text to see how layouts cope.
- **Replace placeholders early** with real content where you can; real words often need more or less space than expected.
- **Keep HTML tags off** for Word, Google Docs, Figma and slide apps.
- **Watch for forgotten placeholders** before publishing; search pages for "lorem".

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing Lorem Ipsum by accident,** which looks unprofessional and confuses visitors and search engines.
- **Designing only with perfect-length text,** then finding real titles are much longer.
- **Using placeholder text in user testing,** where people need real words to judge a page.

## A little history

The text comes from "De finibus bonorum et malorum", written by Cicero in 45 BC. A printer scrambled it into type specimens centuries ago, and it has been the standard dummy text in printing and design ever since.
