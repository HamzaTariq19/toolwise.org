## When to use it

Use this tool to write numbers out in English words. It is handy for writing checks, legal and financial documents that need amounts in words, invoices, formal letters, wedding invitations, and teaching children how to read and write large numbers.

## How to use it

1. Type the **Number**, such as 1,234.56. Commas and spaces are ignored.
2. Choose how to **Write as**:
   - **Words**
   - **Check / cheque amount**
   - **Ordinal (first, second…)**
   - **Decimals read digit by digit**
3. For checks, choose the **Currency**: dollars, pounds, euros, rupees, or no currency for plain whole and fractional parts.
4. Tick **Use "and"** for British style, and **Capitalize** if needed.
5. Read the result under **In words** and click **Copy**.

## Worked examples

- **Words:** 1,234.56 becomes one thousand two hundred thirty-four and fifty-six hundredths.
- **Check:** the same number becomes one thousand two hundred thirty-four dollars and fifty-six cents. With **Capitalize**, every word starts with a capital, as many banks prefer: One Thousand Two Hundred Thirty-Four Dollars And Fifty-Six Cents.
- **Ordinal:** 21 becomes Twenty-first, useful for dates and rankings.
- **British style:** 105 with "and" becomes one hundred and five.

## Writing rules worth knowing

- Numbers from 21 to 99 take a hyphen: forty-two, ninety-nine.
- Large numbers group in thousands: one million two hundred thousand.
- In formal writing, numbers at the start of a sentence are written in words.

## Filling in a check step by step

1. Write the amount in figures in the box, such as 1,234.56.
2. Copy the words from the tool onto the long line.
3. Draw a line through any empty space after the words.
4. Check that the figures and words match before you sign.

## Tips

- **Match the figures and the words on checks.** If they differ, the bank may refuse the check or use the words.
- **Copy rather than retype.** It avoids spelling mistakes like "fourty" (the correct spelling is forty).
- **Use digit by digit for codes and decimals read aloud.** 3.14 becomes three point one four.
- **Use ordinals for dates in invitations.** The twenty-first of June reads more formally than 21 June.

## Common mistakes

- **Spelling forty with a u.** Four has a u, but forty does not.
- **Mixing styles.** Use "and" consistently, or not at all, within one document.
- **Forgetting the cents on checks.** Even zero cents should be written, such as "and zero cents" or "and 00/100".
