## What this tool does

The word frequency counter lists every word in your text with how many times it appears, sorted from most to least frequent. It can also count repeated two- and three-word phrases. Use it to spot words you overuse, find the main themes of a document, check keyword usage, or analyze texts for language study and research.

## How to count word frequency

1. Paste your text into the box.
2. Choose whether to **hide common words** and **include numbers**.
3. Pick **Phrases of 1, 2 or 3 words**.
4. Read the table: rank, word, count and share of the text.
5. Press **Download CSV** to open the full list in a spreadsheet.

## Ideas for using it

- **Editing:** discover crutch words like "really", "very", "just" or "actually" and cut them.
- **SEO and content writing:** check which terms dominate an article. For keyword-focused analysis, also see the [Keyword Density Checker](/keyword-density-checker).
- **Students and researchers:** compare vocabulary across texts, speeches or authors.
- **Language learners:** find the most useful words to learn from a book or article.
- **Customer feedback:** paste survey answers and see which words people mention most.

## Reading the results

High counts for content words tell you what a text is about. If a single word appears far more often than any other, consider using synonyms or restructuring sentences. In phrase mode, repeated phrases like "in order to" or "it is important to" often signal wordiness.

## How words are counted

Words are sequences of letters and numbers, so "don't" and "well-known" each count as one word. Counting ignores capital letters, so "The" and "the" are combined. Phrases are counted across the whole text, and a phrase is skipped if it starts or ends with a common word when common words are hidden.
