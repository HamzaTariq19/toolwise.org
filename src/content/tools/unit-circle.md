  - q: What is the unit circle?
    a: A circle with radius 1 centered at the origin. For any angle measured from the positive x-axis, the point where the angle meets the circle has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).
  - q: How do I convert degrees to radians?
    a: Multiply by π/180. So 90° is π/2, 60° is π/3 and 180° is π. To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/π.
  - q: Why is tan 90° undefined?
    a: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, and cos 90° = 0. Dividing by zero is undefined, which is why the tangent graph has vertical asymptotes at 90° and 270°.
  - q: What is a reference angle?
    a: The acute angle between the terminal side of your angle and the x-axis. Sine, cosine and tangent of any angle equal those of its reference angle, with the sign set by the quadrant.

## What this tool does

The interactive unit circle shows how angles relate to sine, cosine and tangent. Drag the point around the circle, use the slider, type an angle or tap one of the special angles. The tool shows the angle in degrees and radians, the exact values of cos θ, sin θ and tan θ (such as √3/2), their decimals, the quadrant and the reference angle. The blue line is the cosine (x) and the red line is the sine (y).

## How to use it

- **Drag the point** around the circle. With **Snap to special angles** on, it clicks to 30°, 45°, 60° and so on when close.
- **Type an angle** in degrees, including negative angles or angles over 360°; the tool shows the coterminal angle.
- Switch the circle labels between **Degrees** and **Radians**.
- Tick **Show tangent line** to see tan θ as a length on the line x = 1.
- Tap a row in the **Special angles** table to jump to that angle.

## Remembering the special values

For angles in the first quadrant, the sine values follow a simple pattern:

- sin 0° = √0/2 = 0
- sin 30° = √1/2 = 1/2
- sin 45° = √2/2
- sin 60° = √3/2
- sin 90° = √4/2 = 1

Cosine runs the same pattern in reverse. Tangent is sine divided by cosine.

## Signs in each quadrant

The mnemonic **"All Students Take Calculus"** gives which functions are positive:

- **Quadrant I** (0°–90°): all are positive.
- **Quadrant II** (90°–180°): sine is positive.
- **Quadrant III** (180°–270°): tangent is positive.
- **Quadrant IV** (270°–360°): cosine is positive.

## Why radians?

A radian is the angle that makes an arc as long as the radius. A full turn is 2π radians. Radians make many formulas in calculus and physics simpler, which is why they're used in advanced math.
