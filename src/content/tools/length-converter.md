## When to use it

Use this converter whenever you need to switch between metric and imperial lengths. It is handy for reading furniture sizes from another country, following a sewing or DIY plan, checking a running distance, working out a child's height in the other system, or doing science homework with micrometers and kilometers.

## How to use it

1. Type a number in **Value**.
2. Choose the unit you have under the value, such as **Centimeters (cm)**.
3. Choose the unit you want under **Result**, such as **Inches (in)**.
4. Read the answer in the result box and in the line below it.
5. Use the swap button between the boxes to reverse the conversion, or **Copy result** to copy just the number.

The **All conversions** table below shows your value in every unit at once: millimeters, centimeters, meters, kilometers, inches, feet, yards, miles, nautical miles and micrometers.

## Worked example

A desk is listed as 100 cm wide and you think in inches. Enter 100, choose centimeters and inches, and the result is 39.37007874 in, a little over 39⅓ inches. The table also shows 3.280839895 ft, so the desk is just under 3 feet 4 inches.

Going the other way, a 6-foot shelf is 6 × 30.48 = 182.88 cm. Swap the units and enter 6 with feet selected to check.

## Useful numbers to remember

- 1 inch = 2.54 cm
- 1 foot = 30.48 cm
- 1 yard = 0.9144 m
- 1 mile = 1.609344 km
- 1 km = about 0.62 miles

## Very small and very large lengths

Micrometers are used for things like the thickness of hair or paper: a sheet of office paper is about 100 µm. For long distances, kilometers and miles are easier to read than meters, so pick the unit that keeps the number simple.

## Tips

- **Round sensibly.** Measuring tapes rarely go finer than a millimeter or a sixteenth of an inch, so there is no point keeping eight decimal places.
- **Use the table for quick comparisons.** It is faster than changing the to-unit several times.
- **Check the unit before you cut.** In DIY, a mix-up between inches and centimeters is an expensive mistake.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating 5.9 feet as 5 ft 9 in.** 5.9 feet is 5 ft 10.8 in. Convert feet and inches separately.
- **Confusing miles and nautical miles.** Boat and flight distances are usually nautical.
- **Mixing up thousands and decimals.** The tool reads 1,250 as one thousand two hundred and fifty, and 12,5 as twelve and a half. If in doubt, type plain digits with a dot, such as 1250 or 12.5.
