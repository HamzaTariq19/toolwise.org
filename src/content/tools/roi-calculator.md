  - q: How is ROI calculated?
    a: ROI equals the gain or loss divided by the amount invested, times 100. If you invest 10,000 and get back 13,500, the ROI is 3,500 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 35%.
  - q: What is annualized return?
    a: Annualized return, or CAGR (compound annual growth rate), is the steady yearly return that would turn your starting amount into the final amount over the period. It lets you compare investments held for different lengths of time.
  - q: Should I include fees and dividends?
    a: Yes. For a true picture, subtract fees and taxes from the final value and add any dividends, interest or rent you received.
  - q: Is a higher ROI always better?
    a: Not on its own. Consider the time it took, the risk involved and how easily you can get your money back. A 30% return over ten years is far less impressive than 30% in one year.

## What this calculator does

The ROI calculator measures how well an investment performed. Enter what you put in and what you got back, and it shows the return on investment as a percentage, the profit or loss in money, and how many times your money multiplied. Add the investment period to see the annualized return, which makes it easy to compare investments of different lengths.

## How to use it

1. Enter the **amount invested**, including purchase costs.
2. Enter the **amount returned**, the final value plus any income received.
3. Optionally enter the **investment period** in years.
4. Read your ROI, profit or loss, annualized return and value multiple.

## Examples

- Invest 10,000, receive 13,500 after 3 years: ROI **35%**, annualized return about **10.5% a year**.
- Invest 2,000 in marketing that brings 5,000 in profit: ROI **150%**.
- Buy shares for 8,000 and sell for 6,000: ROI **−25%**, a loss of 2,000.

## Where ROI is used

- **Investing:** shares, funds, property and other assets.
- **Business:** marketing campaigns, new equipment, software and training.
- **Personal decisions:** home improvements, solar panels or courses that raise earnings.

## Limitations of ROI

- **Time is ignored** in basic ROI. Always look at the annualized return for longer investments.
- **Risk is ignored.** A risky investment may need a higher return to be worthwhile.
- **Cash flows during the period** (adding or withdrawing money) are not handled by this simple calculation.
- **Inflation:** a 20% return over five years may be modest once inflation is considered; use the [Inflation Calculator](/inflation-calculator) to compare.
