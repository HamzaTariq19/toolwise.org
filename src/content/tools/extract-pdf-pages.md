## What this tool does

Extract PDF Pages creates a new PDF that contains only the pages you choose. Use it to send someone just the relevant section of a long report, keep the pages of a manual you actually use, pull a single invoice out of a monthly statement, or save one chapter of a course pack.

## How to extract pages from a PDF

1. Tap **Choose PDF file** and pick your document.
2. Tap the pages you want. Selected pages get a blue tick.
3. Or type page numbers such as `3, 8-12` and press **Select**. The quick buttons select all, odd or even pages.
4. Press **Extract pages**. The button shows how many pages will be included.
5. Download your new PDF.

## Selecting pages quickly

- **Ranges:** `10-20` selects eleven pages in one go.
- **Mixed:** `1, 3, 5-7` selects pages 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7.
- **To the end:** `15-end` selects page 15 and everything after it.
- **Odd and even:** helpful for double-sided scans, where all the fronts are odd pages and the backs are even pages.

If you type a page that does not exist, toolwise tells you how many pages the PDF has.

## Extract, split or delete?

- Use **Extract** to make one new file from some pages.
- Use [Split PDF](/split-pdf) to cut a document into several separate files.
- Use [Delete PDF Pages](/delete-pdf-pages) when it is easier to say what you don't want.

## Quality and file size

Pages are copied as they are, without converting or compressing them. Text remains selectable and searchable, and images keep their original quality. The new file is usually much smaller than the original because only the content of the chosen pages is included. To shrink it further, use [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf).

## Sharing just what is needed

Sending only the pages someone actually needs is good practice for privacy. A landlord asking for proof of income needs the summary page of a payslip, not your full year of statements. Extracting the relevant pages keeps unrelated personal details out of other people's inboxes and makes your email attachments smaller and quicker to open.

## Private by design

Your PDF is read and rewritten inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded, which makes the tool safe for bank statements, legal papers and anything else confidential.
