## When to use it

Use the image compressor whenever a photo is bigger than it needs to be: a form rejects your upload, an email bounces because the attachment is too large, your website loads slowly, or your phone is running out of space. Photos straight from a phone are often 3 to 8 MB because they are saved at very high quality and full camera resolution. Most of that data is invisible on a screen, so it can be removed safely.

## How to compress images

1. Tap **Choose images**, or drag JPG, PNG or WebP files onto the box. You can pick several at once.
2. The images are compressed straight away. Each one is listed with its original size, new size and the percentage saved.
3. Adjust the **Settings** if you want. Changes apply instantly, so there is no need to start again:
   - **Quality** sets the balance between file size and detail.
   - **Save as** keeps the original format or switches to JPG, WebP or PNG.
   - **Maximum size** shrinks the dimensions, for example to 1920 px (Full HD).
4. Press **Download**. With more than one image you get a single ZIP file.
5. Press **Start over** to compress a different set.

## A worked example

Say you have a 4.2 MB photo from your phone, 4032 × 3024 pixels, and a job application site allows uploads of 1 MB. At the default 75% quality the file drops to about 1.3 MB, still too big. Set **Maximum size** to 1920 px and it falls to roughly 380 KB. The picture still looks sharp on any screen, and you are well under the limit.

## Tips for the best results

- **Reduce the pixels first.** Shrinking the dimensions saves far more space than lowering quality, and a 1920 px wide photo is enough for screens, emails and most websites.
- **Use WebP for websites.** It is usually 25 to 35% smaller than JPG at the same visible quality and works in every modern browser.
- **Keep JPG for documents and forms.** Some older systems and official portals still refuse WebP.
- **Stay between 70 and 80% quality** for photos you care about; go to 50 to 60% only for thumbnails and previews.

## Common mistakes

- **Compressing the same photo again and again.** Each round loses a little detail. Always start from the original file.
- **Saving photos as PNG.** PNG files of photographs are often five times larger than JPG with no visible benefit.
- **Deleting the originals.** Keep the full-quality versions if you might want to print or crop the photos later.
- **Choosing a tiny maximum size for printing.** For prints, keep at least 2500 px on the long side.

## Private by design

Your images are processed by your own browser. They are never uploaded, so the compressor is safe for personal photos, ID scans and unreleased work.
