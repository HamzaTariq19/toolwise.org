## When to use it

Use this calculator when you are pricing a product or checking whether a sale is worth it. It works out profit margin, markup and profit per item from whichever numbers you already know. Shop owners, freelancers and online sellers use it to set prices that cover costs and leave a healthy profit.

## How to use it

1. Choose your **Currency**.
2. Under **I know**, pick one of three options:
   - **Cost and price** if you already have a selling price.
   - **Cost and target margin** to find the price that gives a set margin.
   - **Cost and markup** to find the price after adding a markup.
3. Enter the **Cost**, what the item costs you to buy or make.
4. Enter the **Selling price**, **Target profit margin** or **Markup**, depending on your choice.
5. Read the **Profit margin**, **Selling price**, **Profit per item**, **Markup** and **Cost**.

## Worked example

You buy a lamp for $40 and sell it for $65. The profit per item is $25. The profit margin is 38.46%, because $25 is 38.46% of $65. The markup is 62.5%, because $25 is 62.5% of the $40 cost.

Now say you want a 35% margin. Switch to **Cost and target margin** and enter 35. The tool tells you to charge $61.54, which gives a profit of $21.54.

If you prefer to add a 50% markup instead, choose **Cost and markup**. The price becomes $60, and the margin works out at 33.33%.

## Tips

- **Decide which number you mean.** Suppliers and shops often talk about markup, while accountants talk about margin. Mixing them up is the most common pricing error.
- **Leave room for discounts.** If you plan to run 20% off sales, check the margin at the sale price too.
- **Check against fees.** Marketplaces and card payments take a cut. Add these to the cost for a true margin.

## Quick rule

Margin is always smaller than markup for the same item. A 50% margin needs a 100% markup, which means charging double the cost.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding a 30% markup to get a 30% margin.** A 30% markup only gives a 23% margin. To get 30% margin, use **Cost and target margin**.
- **Leaving out shipping.** If you offer free delivery, the postage is part of your cost.
- **Using prices that include tax.** Remove the tax first with the [Sales Tax Calculator](/sales-tax-calculator), or the margin will look larger than it is.
