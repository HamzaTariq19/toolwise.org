  - q: What is the difference between margin and markup?
    a: Both compare profit, but against different numbers. Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Markup is profit as a share of the cost. An item costing 40 and selling for 60 has a 33.3% margin and a 50% markup.
  - q: How do I price for a target margin?
    a: Divide the cost by (1 − margin). For a 35% margin on a 40 cost, the price is 40 ÷ 0.65 = 61.54. Choose "Cost and target margin" in the calculator to do this automatically.
  - q: Why can't a margin be 100% or more?
    a: Margin is profit divided by the selling price, and profit can never be larger than the price unless the cost is negative. Markup, on the other hand, can be any size, such as 200%.

    a: Gross margin, based on the direct cost of the item. Net margin also subtracts rent, wages, marketing and other overheads, so it is lower.

## What this calculator does

The profit margin calculator helps you understand and set prices. Enter what an item costs you and what you sell it for, and it calculates your profit per item, profit margin and markup. Or work backwards: enter the cost and a target margin or markup, and it tells you what price to charge.

## How to use it

1. Choose what you know: **cost and price**, **cost and target margin**, or **cost and markup**.
2. Enter the **cost**: what you pay to buy or make the item.
3. Enter the selling price, target margin or markup.
4. Read the margin, selling price, profit and markup.

## Formulas

- **Profit** = price − cost
- **Margin** = profit ÷ price × 100
- **Markup** = profit ÷ cost × 100
- **Price for a margin** = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100)
- **Price for a markup** = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100)

## Margin and markup conversions

- 20% markup = 16.7% margin
- 25% markup = 20% margin
- 50% markup = 33.3% margin
- 100% markup = 50% margin

Mixing up the two is a common pricing mistake: if you want a 50% margin but add a 50% markup, you only get a 33% margin.

## Pricing tips

- **Know your full costs.** Include packaging, shipping, payment fees and platform commissions in the cost.
- **Check margins before discounting.** A sale can wipe out profit; see the [Discount Calculator](/discount-calculator).
- **Compare with your market.** A healthy margin varies by industry: groceries run thin margins, while software and services are often high.
- **Review regularly.** Supplier price rises quietly eat into margins.
