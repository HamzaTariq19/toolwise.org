## When to use it

Use this calculator when you want a clear plan to clear a credit card balance. It answers two questions: how long will it take if I pay a fixed amount each month, and how much do I need to pay to be debt-free by a certain date? It also shows how much interest you will pay along the way, which is often the number that motivates people to pay more.

## How to use it

1. Choose your **Currency**.
2. Enter your **Card balance** and the **Interest rate (APR)** from your statement.
3. Under **I want to**, pick **Pay a fixed amount** or **Be debt-free by**.
4. Enter your **Monthly payment**, or the number of **Months to pay it off**.
5. Read the results: **Debt-free in**, **Total interest**, **Total paid** and the **Debt-free date**.

In fixed-payment mode, the tool also shows what happens if you pay 25% more each month, so you can see the saving straight away.

## Worked example

You owe $5,000 at 22.9% APR and can pay $250 a month. The card will be clear in 2 years and 2 months (26 payments). You will pay $1,358.22 in interest, so $6,358.22 in total.

The tool then suggests paying $312.50 a month instead. That clears the card in 1 year and 8 months and saves $334.90 in interest.

Switch to **Be debt-free by** and enter 24 months. The tool shows you need about $261.62 a month, with around $1,279 of interest in total.

## Tips

- **Pay a fixed amount, not the minimum.** Set up a standing payment for the same sum every month, even as the balance falls.
- **Round up.** Small increases make a big difference at credit card rates. Try a few amounts and watch the total interest change.
- **Stop adding to the balance.** Use a debit card or cash while you pay the card down.
- **Ask for a lower rate.** Card companies sometimes agree, especially if you have paid on time.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong rate.** Cash advances and expired promotional offers often carry a higher rate than purchases.
- **Forgetting fees.** Late fees and annual fees add to the balance. Paying on time avoids late fees and can protect a promotional rate.
- **Comparing payments, not totals.** A lower monthly payment feels easier, but it nearly always means more interest overall. Look at **Total paid** before you decide.

Results are estimates. Your card company works out interest daily and may round differently, so your actual payoff date can differ by a month or so.
