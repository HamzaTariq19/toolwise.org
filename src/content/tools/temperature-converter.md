## When to use it

Use this converter to switch between Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin and Rankine. It is useful when following a recipe from another country, reading a weather forecast abroad, setting an oven, checking a fever reading, or doing science homework that uses Kelvin.

## How to use it

1. Enter the temperature in **Value**. Negative numbers are fine, such as −5.
2. Choose the scale you have, such as **Celsius (°C)**.
3. Choose the scale you want, such as **Fahrenheit (°F)**.
4. Read the result, or click **Copy result**.
5. Use the swap button to convert the other way.

The **All conversions** table shows the same temperature in all four scales at once.

## Worked example

A weather forecast for Lisbon says 20 °C and you are used to Fahrenheit. Enter 20 with Celsius and Fahrenheit selected. The result is 68 °F, a mild spring day. The table also shows 293.15 K and 527.67 °R.

Now an American recipe says to bake at 350 °F. Swap the units, enter 350, and the result is about 176.7 °C. Most ovens have marks every 10 degrees, so set it to 180 °C, or 160 °C for a fan oven.

## Handy reference points

- Water freezes at 0 °C, 32 °F or 273.15 K.
- Body temperature is about 37 °C, or 98.6 °F.
- Water boils at 100 °C, or 212 °F, at sea level.
- A warm summer day of 30 °C is 86 °F.

## Fever and health readings

A normal body temperature is about 36.1 to 37.2 °C (97 to 99 °F). Many guides treat 38 °C, or 100.4 °F, as a fever. If a thermometer reads in a unit you are not used to, convert it here, and follow your doctor's advice on what to do next.

## Tips

- **Remember fan ovens.** Fan or convection ovens usually need about 20 °C (25 °F) less than recipes written for conventional ovens.
- **Watch the minus sign.** −10 °C is 14 °F, while 10 °C is 50 °F.
- **Use Kelvin without a degree sign.** Scientists write 300 K, not 300 °K.

## Common mistakes

- **Converting a temperature difference like a temperature.** A rise of 10 °C is a rise of 18 °F, not 50 °F, because the +32 only applies to actual temperatures.
- **Forgetting to round oven settings.** Ovens are not that precise, so the nearest 5 or 10 degrees is fine.
- **Mixing up °F and °C on thermometers.** Many show both scales, so check which side you are reading.
