## What this converter does

The temperature converter switches temperatures between Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin and Rankine. It's useful for weather, cooking, travel, health and science, and it shows all four scales at once.

## How to use it

1. Type the **temperature**. Negative numbers are fine.
2. Choose the scale you have and the scale you want.
3. Read the result and the other scales in the table.

## Formulas

- **°C to °F:** °F = °C × 9/5 + 32
- **°F to °C:** °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
- **°C to K:** K = °C + 273.15
- **°F to °R:** °R = °F + 459.67

## Useful reference temperatures

- Water freezes: 0 °C = 32 °F = 273.15 K
- Room temperature: about 20 °C = 68 °F
- Normal body temperature: about 37 °C = 98.6 °F
- Fever: from about 38 °C = 100.4 °F
- Water boils at sea level: 100 °C = 212 °F

## Oven temperatures

- 150 °C ≈ 300 °F (slow)
- 180 °C ≈ 350 °F (moderate, most cakes)
- 200 °C ≈ 400 °F (hot, roasting)
- 220 °C ≈ 425 °F (very hot)

Fan (convection) ovens usually need about 20 °C (25 °F) less than conventional ovens.

## About the scales

Celsius is used in most of the world and in science. Fahrenheit is used mainly in the United States. Kelvin is the scientific scale that starts at absolute zero, so it has no negative values and no degree sign. Rankine is the Fahrenheit equivalent of Kelvin, used in some US engineering fields.
