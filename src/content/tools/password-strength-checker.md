## What this tool does

The password strength checker estimates how hard your password would be to guess. As you type, it rates the password from **Very weak** to **Very strong**, shows how long it might take to crack in different attack situations, and highlights what an attacker would notice: common passwords, dictionary words and names, sequences like 1234, repeated characters, keyboard patterns like qwerty, and years or dates. Practical tips explain how to make it stronger.

## Your password stays on your device

The check runs entirely in your browser. The password isn't sent over the internet, isn't stored, and is cleared from the box when you leave the page. This page also loads no advertising or analytics scripts. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it keeps working.

## How to use it

1. Type a password. Use the eye button to show or hide it.
2. Read the rating and the crack time estimates.
3. Check **What an attacker would notice** for weak parts.
4. Follow the tips, and try again until it's rated **Strong** or **Very strong**.

## What makes a strong password

- **Length:** 12 characters at minimum, and 14 or more for important accounts.
- **Unpredictability:** avoid names, birthdays, pets, sports teams and keyboard patterns.
- **Passphrases** of four or more random words, such as "cactus orbit pillow lantern", are long, strong and easy to remember.
- **Uniqueness:** never reuse a password. If one site is breached, attackers try the same password everywhere else.

## Beyond passwords

Turn on **two-step verification** (2FA) wherever you can, and use a **password manager** to create and remember unique passwords for every site. Then you only need to remember one strong master password.
