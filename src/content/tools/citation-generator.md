## When to use it

The citation generator formats references for books, websites and journal articles in APA 7, MLA 9 and Chicago style. Use it while writing an essay, report, dissertation or presentation to get the punctuation, order and italics right, and to build a sorted bibliography as you go. It's especially helpful when you have to switch styles between classes.

## How to create a citation

1. Choose the **Style**: **APA 7**, **MLA 9** or **Chicago**.
2. Choose the **Source type**: **Website**, **Book** or **Journal article**.
3. Enter the **Authors** (first and last names). Press **Add author** for more, or leave them empty if there's no author.
4. Fill in the fields shown for that source: **Page or article title**, **Website name** or **Publisher**, **Edition**, **Volume**, **Issue**, **Pages**, **Year published**, **URL** and **Date accessed**.
5. Copy the result under **Your citation** with **Copy citation**. The matching in-text citation is shown underneath.
6. Press **Add to bibliography** to keep it. The **Bibliography** is sorted A–Z; use **Copy all** to paste it into your document.

## A worked example

You're citing Carol Dweck's book *Mindset*, published by Random House in 2006. Choose **Book**, enter Carol S. Dweck, the title "Mindset: The new psychology of success", the publisher and the year.

- **APA 7:** Dweck, C. S. (2006). *Mindset: The new psychology of success*. Random House. In-text: (Dweck, 2006)
- **MLA 9:** Dweck, Carol S. *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*. Random House, 2006. In-text: (Dweck 12) when quoting page 12.
- **Chicago:** Dweck, Carol S. *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*. Random House, 2006. In-text (author-date): (Dweck 2006)

## Tips

- **Collect details as you research:** author, title, date, publisher or website, and URL.
- **Use the publication's own spelling of names** and include middle initials when given.
- **Check italics after pasting.** Titles should stay italic in your document; paste with formatting kept.
- **Add a page number** to in-text citations when you quote directly.
- **Use a hanging indent** in your reference list (second and later lines indented), as most styles require.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing styles** in one paper, such as APA in-text citations with an MLA reference list.
- **Citing a search engine or database** instead of the actual article or page.
- **Leaving out the year,** or using the year you read it instead of when it was published.
- **Pasting long tracking links;** use a clean URL or the DOI for journal articles.
