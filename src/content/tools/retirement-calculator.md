## When to use it

The retirement calculator estimates how much you could have saved by the time you retire, what that's worth in today's money, and the monthly income it might provide. Use it to check whether you're on track, see how much difference saving a little more makes, decide when you could retire, or start planning if you haven't saved yet.

## How to estimate your retirement savings

1. Choose your **Currency**.
2. Enter **Your age** and your planned **Retirement age**.
3. Enter your **Retirement savings today** and how much **You save each month**.
4. Set the **Investment return / year**, **Inflation / year** and the **Yearly withdrawal rate in retirement**.
5. Read your **Savings at retirement**, its value **In today's money**, the **Monthly income it could provide** (also in today's money), and how much comes from your contributions versus **Investment growth**.

## A worked example

You're 30, have $20,000 saved and put away $500 a month. With a 6% return, 2.5% inflation and a 4% withdrawal rate, you'd have about **$840,524** at 65. In today's money that's about **$354,172**. It could provide around **$2,802 a month**, or **$1,181 a month** in today's money. You'd contribute **$230,000** yourself; **$610,524** would come from investment growth.

## The power of starting early

In that example, about two-thirds of the final pot builds up in the last 15 years, because returns build on earlier returns. Starting five years earlier often makes a bigger difference than saving much more later. If you're starting late, increasing contributions and working a little longer both help significantly.

## Tips

- **Use employer matching** if your workplace offers it; it's free money.
- **Increase savings with each pay rise,** even by 1%.
- **Check fees:** a 1% yearly fee can reduce your final pot by about a fifth over 35 years.
- **Revisit the plan yearly** with your actual savings.

## Testing scenarios

Try the same plan with returns of 4%, 6% and 8% to see a cautious, middle and hopeful outcome. Then change one thing at a time, such as saving $100 more a month or retiring two years later, to see which makes the biggest difference for you.

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring inflation,** which makes the future pot look bigger than it really is.
- **Using a very high return** that markets may not deliver.
- **Forgetting other income,** such as state pensions, or other costs, such as healthcare.
