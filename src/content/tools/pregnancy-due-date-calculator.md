## When to use it

The pregnancy due date calculator estimates when your baby is due and shows a simple timeline of key milestones: the likely conception date, when a test could turn positive, the end of the first trimester, the anatomy scan window, the start of the third trimester and full term. Use it after a positive test to plan appointments, work leave and conversations with family, and to understand how far along you are.

## How to calculate your due date

1. Under **Calculate based on**, choose:
   - **First day of last period** (the most common method).
   - **Conception date**, if you know it.
   - **IVF: 5-day embryo transfer** or **IVF: 3-day embryo transfer**.
2. Enter the date asked for.
3. For the period method, set your **Average cycle length** (most cycles are 21 to 35 days; use 28 if you're not sure).
4. Read the **Estimated due date**, how many days are left and how many weeks pregnant you are today.
5. Check the timeline below for each milestone.

## A worked example

Your last period started on 1 March 2026 and your cycles are usually 28 days. The calculator adds 280 days (40 weeks) and gives an estimated due date of **Sunday, 6 December 2026**. The timeline shows conception around 15 March, a positive test possible from about 29 March, the end of the first trimester on 6 June, and full term (39 weeks) from 29 November. If your cycles are usually 32 days, the due date moves to **10 December**.

## How the dates are worked out

The standard method, called Naegele's rule, adds 280 days to the first day of the last period, assuming a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. For other cycle lengths, the date shifts by the difference. From a conception date the calculator adds 266 days, and for IVF it counts back from the embryo's known age at transfer.

## Tips

- **Write down period start dates** each month; it makes the estimate more reliable.
- **Book an early scan** if your cycles are irregular or you're unsure of the date.
- **Plan around a window, not a single day.** Most babies come between 37 and 42 weeks.
- **Share the timeline** with your partner or family so everyone knows the milestones.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the last day of the period** instead of the first.
- **Ignoring cycle length.** A long cycle can move the due date by a week or more.
- **Treating weeks as months.** Pregnancy is about 40 weeks, a little over nine months.
- **Replacing medical advice.** Your midwife or doctor's dating scan takes priority.
