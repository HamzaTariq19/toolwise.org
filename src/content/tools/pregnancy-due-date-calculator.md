## What this calculator does

The pregnancy due date calculator estimates when your baby is likely to arrive. It also shows how many weeks and days pregnant you are today, which trimester you are in, and the dates of key milestones such as the end of the first trimester and the usual window for the mid-pregnancy anatomy scan. You can calculate from the first day of your last period, from a known conception date, or from an IVF embryo transfer date.

## How to use it

1. Choose what to calculate from. Most people use **First day of last period**.
2. Pick the date.
3. If you used your last period, enter your **average cycle length**. Leave it at 28 days if you are not sure.
4. Your estimated due date, current week of pregnancy and timeline appear instantly.

## Understanding the timeline

- **First trimester (weeks 1 to 13):** the baby's organs form. Many people experience tiredness and nausea.
- **Second trimester (weeks 14 to 27):** often the most comfortable stage. The anatomy scan usually happens between weeks 18 and 21.
- **Third trimester (week 28 to birth):** the baby grows quickly and gets ready for birth. A pregnancy is considered full term from 39 weeks.

## What if my cycle is irregular?

The calculation assumes ovulation happens about 14 days before your next period. If your cycles vary a lot from month to month, the estimate may be less accurate. In that case an early ultrasound is the best way to date the pregnancy, and your midwife or doctor may give you an adjusted due date.

## Planning ahead

Knowing your approximate due date helps you plan appointments, parental leave and preparations. Remember that it is a guide, not a deadline: babies decide for themselves. You might also find our [Date Difference Calculator](/date-difference-calculator) handy for counting days to other events.

## Important note

This calculator gives general estimates for information only. It is not medical advice. Always follow the guidance of your doctor, midwife or other qualified health professional about your pregnancy.
