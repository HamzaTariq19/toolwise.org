## When to use it

The GPA calculator works out your grade point average from letter grades and credit hours. Use it to see where you stand mid-semester, to check whether you'll keep a scholarship or honors standing, to plan which grades you need for a target GPA, or to estimate your cumulative GPA before transcripts are released.

## How to calculate your GPA

1. Choose the **Scale**: **4.0 (A+ = 4.0)** or **4.3 (A+ = 4.3)**.
2. For each course under **Your courses**, enter an optional name, choose the **Grade** and type the **Credits**.
3. Press **Add course** for more rows, or the ✕ button to remove one.
4. Read your **Semester GPA** and the total credits on the right.
5. To include earlier terms, open **Include previous semesters** and enter your **Current GPA** and **Credits completed**. Your **Cumulative GPA** appears.

Your courses are saved in this browser, so you can update grades as they come in.

## A worked example

This semester you have three courses: an A in a 3-credit course, a B+ in another 3-credit course, and an A- in a 4-credit lab course. That's (4.0 × 3) + (3.3 × 3) + (3.7 × 4) = 36.7 grade points over 10 credits, a **semester GPA of 3.67**. If you already have a 3.20 GPA from 45 earlier credits, your **cumulative GPA** becomes (3.20 × 45 + 36.7) ÷ 55 = **3.29**.

## Grade points on the 4.0 scale

- A+ and A: 4.0 · A-: 3.7
- B+: 3.3 · B: 3.0 · B-: 2.7
- C+: 2.3 · C: 2.0 · C-: 1.7
- D+: 1.3 · D: 1.0 · D-: 0.7 · F: 0

## Tips

- **Focus on high-credit courses.** A grade in a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than the same grade in a 1-credit course.
- **Plan backwards.** Try different grades for remaining courses to see what you need for a target.
- **Raising a cumulative GPA takes time.** The more credits you've completed, the less one semester changes it.
- **Check your school's rules** for repeated courses; some replace the old grade.

## Common mistakes

- **Averaging grades without credits,** which treats a 1-credit seminar like a 4-credit core course.
- **Choosing the wrong scale.** If your school doesn't award 4.3 for an A+, use 4.0.
- **Including pass/fail courses** that don't carry grade points.
- **Forgetting a course,** which can make the result look higher than it is.
