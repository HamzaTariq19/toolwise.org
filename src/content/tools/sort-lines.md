## When to use it

Sort Lines puts a list in order: alphabetically, by number, by length, reversed or shuffled. Use it to alphabetise names for a class list or guest list, order prices or scores from highest to lowest, sort keywords, tidy a bibliography, randomise the order of quiz questions or presentation slots, or flip a list that's upside down.

## How to sort lines

1. Paste your list into **Your text**, one item per line, or press **Example**.
2. Choose how to **Sort**:
   - **A to Z** or **Z to A**
   - **Numbers, smallest first** or **Numbers, largest first**
   - **Shortest first** or **Longest first**
   - **Reverse current order** or **Random shuffle**
3. Tick **Ignore capital letters** or **Remove empty lines** if needed.
4. Read the **Sorted lines**, then press **Copy result** or **Download .txt**.

## A worked example

You have the results of a fun run as lines like "Maya – 24:31", "Leo – 22:05" and "Sam – 27:48". Choose **Numbers, smallest first**: the tool uses the first number on each line (the minutes), so Leo, Maya and Sam appear in finishing order. For an alphabetical list of runners instead, choose **A to Z**.

## Tips

- **Put the value you sort by first** in each line when sorting numbers, such as "24:31 Maya".
- **Remove duplicates first** with Remove Duplicate Lines if the list has repeats.
- **Use Random shuffle** for fair speaking orders or to mix quiz questions.
- **Keep a copy of the original order** if it means something, such as sign-up time.

## Sorting by length

**Shortest first** and **Longest first** are handy for spotting outliers: very long lines in a keyword list, the shortest product names for a narrow menu, or empty-looking entries that are really just a few spaces. Lines of equal length keep a natural alphabetical order.

## Common mistakes

- **Sorting several columns** pasted from a spreadsheet; whole lines are sorted, which may not be what you want. Sort in the spreadsheet for multi-column data.
- **Expecting dates to sort correctly in A to Z order** when written as 3/12/2026. Use the year-month-day format (2026-12-03) for reliable sorting.
- **Leaving "Ignore capital letters" off** when a list mixes "apple" and "Banana" and you want them in plain alphabetical order.

## Private by design

Your list is sorted in your browser and never uploaded.
