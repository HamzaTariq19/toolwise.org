## What this tool does

Cut Audio trims an audio file to just the part you need. It shows the recording as a waveform so you can see the quiet and loud parts, lets you drag the start and end handles or type exact times, and plays your selection so you can check it before saving. You can add a smooth **fade in** and **fade out**, then save the clip as **MP3** or **WAV**. Everything happens in your browser, so your recording never leaves your device.

## How to cut an audio file

1. Choose an audio file, or drag it onto the page.
2. Drag the **start** and **end** handles, or type times such as `0:45` or `1:12.5`.
3. Press **Play selection** to listen, and adjust as needed. Tap the waveform to move the playback position, then use the pin buttons to set the start or end there.
4. Pick a fade in and fade out if you want gentle edges.
5. Choose **MP3** or **WAV** and press **Cut audio**.
6. Listen to the result and press **Download**.

## Tips for clean cuts

- **Cut in silence.** Look for flat parts of the waveform between words or notes so the clip doesn't start mid-sound.
- **Use short fades** (0.5 to 1 second) to avoid clicks at the start and end.
- **Use longer fades** (2 to 3 seconds) for music clips and ringtones.
- **Keep a copy** of the original until you're happy with the result.

## Common uses

- Making a ringtone or alarm sound from a song you own.
- Removing silence or chatter from the start and end of a voice memo, podcast or lecture.
- Clipping a quote from an interview.
- Preparing a short sample for a presentation or video.

## MP3 or WAV?

MP3 files are small and play everywhere, so they're ideal for sharing and phones. WAV files are uncompressed and much larger, but keep every detail, which is best if you'll edit the clip further.
