## When to use it

Use this calculator to see how prices have changed over time, or to estimate what something will cost in the future. It is handy for understanding an old salary or price in today's money, checking whether a pay rise kept up with prices, or planning how much a goal will cost years from now.

## How to use it

The tool has two modes under **Calculate with**.

**Historical US inflation**

1. Enter the **Amount (US dollars)**.
2. Choose the year in **In year**.
3. Choose the year to compare with in **Is worth in**.
4. Read the **Equivalent value**, the **Total price change** and the **Average per year**. A fourth figure shows the same comparison the other way round.

You can pick any year from 1913 to the latest year in the data, and the second year can be earlier than the first.

**My own rate**

1. Choose your **Currency**.
2. Enter the **Amount today**, the **Inflation per year** and the **Years from now**.
3. Read what the same things are likely to cost in the future.

## Worked example

You want to know what $100 in 2000 means in 2024 money. Choose 2000 and 2024 and enter 100. The equivalent value is $182.17, so you would need about $182 in 2024 to buy what $100 bought in 2000. Prices rose 82.2% in total, about 2.53% a year on average. Turned the other way, something that cost $100 in 2024 would have cost about $54.90 in 2000.

For the future, choose **My own rate**, enter $1,000, 3% a year and 10 years. The same basket would cost about $1,343.92.

## Tips

- **Compare a pay rise.** Put your old salary in and see what it is worth today. If your current pay is lower than the result, your buying power has fallen.
- **Plan savings goals.** A goal that costs $20,000 today will cost more in ten years. Use the future value as your target.
- **Try several rates.** For future estimates, try 2%, 3% and 5% to see a hopeful, a middle and a cautious case.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the US data for another currency.** Use **My own rate** instead.
- **Treating the average as your own cost of living.** Housing and childcare often rise faster than the overall index.
- **Forgetting that small rates add up.** At 3% a year, prices roughly double in 24 years.
