## What this calculator does

The inflation calculator shows how the buying power of money changes over time. In historical mode, it uses official US consumer price data to tell you what an amount from any year since 1913 would be worth in another year. In custom mode, you enter an inflation rate to estimate what today's prices might be in the future.

## How to use it

**Historical US inflation:**

1. Enter an **amount** in dollars.
2. Choose the **year** the money is from and the year to compare with.
3. Read the equivalent value, the total price change and the average inflation per year.

**My own rate:**

1. Enter an **amount** in any currency.
2. Enter an expected **inflation rate** and the number of **years**.
3. See what the same goods might cost in the future and how buying power shrinks.

## Examples

- **$100 in 2000** buys roughly what **$182 bought in 2024**, because prices rose by about 82%.
- At 3% inflation, something costing **1,000 today** would cost about **1,344 in 10 years**.

## Why inflation matters

- **Savings:** money in an account earning less than inflation slowly loses buying power.
- **Salaries:** a pay rise below inflation is effectively a pay cut.
- **Long-term plans:** retirement and education goals need to account for future prices.
- **History:** comparing prices across decades only makes sense after adjusting for inflation.

## About the data

The Consumer Price Index measures the average change in prices paid by urban consumers for a basket of goods and services, including food, housing, transport and medical care. Your personal inflation can differ from the average, depending on what you buy and where you live. The bundled data currently runs to the most recent full year shown under the result and will be updated as new annual figures are published.
