## When to use it

Use this calculator to estimate how many calories you need each day to keep your weight steady, lose weight or gain weight. It is a sensible starting point for planning meals, setting a goal in a food tracking app, or checking whether your current eating fits your activity level.

## How to use it

1. Choose your **Units**: **Metric (cm, kg)** or **US (ft, lb)**.
2. Choose your **Sex** and enter your **Age**.
3. Enter your **Height** and **Weight**.
4. Choose your **Activity level**, from **Little or no exercise** to **Very active**.
5. Read the calories **To maintain your weight**, your resting needs (BMR), and the table of goals for losing or gaining weight.

## Choosing an activity level

Most people overestimate how active they are. If you sit at a desk and exercise two or three times a week, choose **Light**. Choose **Moderate** only if you exercise most days. **Very active** is for people with physical jobs who also train daily.

## Worked example

A 35-year-old woman is 168 cm tall, weighs 68 kg and exercises 3 to 5 days a week. Her BMR is about 1,394 kcal a day. With the moderate activity level, she needs about 2,161 kcal a day to maintain her weight.

To lose about 0.5 kg a week, the table suggests 1,611 kcal a day. A milder goal of 0.25 kg a week suggests 1,886 kcal, which is easier to keep up. To gain 0.25 kg a week, she would aim for about 2,436 kcal.

## Tips

- **Start with the mild option.** Small changes are easier to stick to and protect muscle.
- **Recalculate as you change.** When your weight changes by a few kilos, enter the new weight for an updated estimate.
- **Focus on food quality too.** Protein, vegetables and whole grains keep you full on fewer calories.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing too high an activity level.** This is the most common reason the estimate seems too high.
- **Eating below your BMR for long periods.** Very low intakes can leave you tired and make it hard to get enough nutrients.
- **Expecting a straight line.** Weight goes up and down with water from day to day. Look at weekly averages.

This is a general estimate. If you are pregnant, have a medical condition or are planning a big change in diet, talk to a doctor or dietitian first.
