## What this tool does

The essay word counter tracks your essay against a word limit. It counts words the way most teachers do and can leave out parts that usually don't count: the **reference list**, **in-text citations** such as (Smith, 2020), and your **title and headings**. A progress bar and a plain message tell you how many words you have left or need to cut, and it estimates how many pages your essay fills.

## How to use it

1. Paste or type your essay.
2. Enter your **word limit** and choose **About (±10%)**, **Maximum** or **Minimum**.
3. Choose what not to count, following your assignment rules.
4. Read the status message under the bar. Your draft stays here if you leave and come back on the same device.

## What gets excluded

- **Reference list:** everything after a heading such as References, Works Cited or Bibliography, as long as it appears after the first part of your essay.
- **In-text citations:** brackets that contain a year, a page number or "n.d.", like (Lee, 2019, p. 4), and numbered citations like [3].
- **Title and headings:** short lines with no full stop at the end, and lines starting with #.

The note below the statistics shows exactly how many words were left out, so you can double-check.

## Hitting your word count

**Too long?**

- Cut filler phrases: "due to the fact that" becomes "because".
- Remove repeated points and long quotes; paraphrase instead.
- Check that every paragraph supports your main argument.

**Too short?**

- Add evidence or an example to each main point.
- Explain why each piece of evidence matters.
- Address a counter-argument and respond to it.

## Pages and time estimates

Page estimates use about 275 words per double-spaced page and 550 per single-spaced page. Reading time uses an average of 238 words per minute, and speaking time 130 words per minute, useful if you have to present your essay.
