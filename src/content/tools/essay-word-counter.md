## When to use it

Use this tool to check an essay, report, dissertation chapter or personal statement against its word limit. Unlike a basic word counter, it can leave out the reference list, in-text citations and headings, which many schools and universities do not count, and it shows pages, paragraphs, sentences and reading time.

## How to use it

1. Paste your essay into **Your essay**.
2. Choose what to leave out: **Don't count the reference list**, **Don't count in-text citations** and **Don't count title and headings**.
3. Enter your **Word limit** and choose the **Limit type**: **About (±10%)**, **Maximum** or **Minimum**.
4. Read the **words counted** and the status message, which tells you if you are on target, over or under.
5. Check **Pages, double spaced**, **Pages, single spaced**, **Paragraphs**, **Sentences**, **Avg. sentence**, **Characters**, **Reading time** and **Speaking time**.

## Worked example

Your history essay has a 1,500-word limit, about ±10%. You paste 1,720 words, including a 160-word reference list and 70 words of citations like (Smith, 2020).

With the reference list excluded, the count is 1,560, which is within the allowed range. If your teacher also excludes citations, tick that box and the count drops to 1,490. The notes under the total show exactly how many words were left out and why, so you can explain your count if asked.

At about 1,500 words, the essay is roughly 5.5 pages double spaced and takes about 6 minutes to read, or 12 minutes to read aloud.

## Planning by word count

Split your limit across sections before you write. For a 1,500-word essay, you might plan 150 words for the introduction, three main sections of about 400 words each, and 150 words for the conclusion. Paste each section as you finish it to see how you are doing.

## Tips

- **Check your course rules.** Some count headings and citations, some do not.
- **Watch the average sentence length.** Above about 25 words, sentences can become hard to follow.
- **Cut, do not pad.** If you are over, remove repetition and filler before cutting arguments.
- **Use speaking time for presentations.** It estimates how long a speech will take at a natural pace.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting the title page and appendix.** Most limits only include the main text.
- **Relying on page counts.** Fonts and margins change pages, so always use the word count for limits.
- **Leaving a reference heading unlabelled.** The tool needs a heading like References on its own line to know where the list starts.
