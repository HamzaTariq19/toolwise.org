## When to use it

Passport Photo Maker turns an ordinary photo into a correctly sized passport or visa photo, and lays out several copies on a standard print sheet. Use it when renewing a passport, applying for a visa, an ID card, a residence permit or a driving licence, and avoid paying for a photo booth. You get the exact size for your country and guide lines that show where the head should be.

## How to make a passport photo

1. Choose the **Photo size** for your document, such as **United States passport or visa: 2 × 2 in** or **United Kingdom: 35 × 45 mm**.
2. Tap **Choose photo**, or drag a clear, front-facing photo (JPG, PNG or HEIC) onto the box.
3. **Drag the photo to move it**, and use **Zoom** (or pinch / scroll) until the face fits inside the oval and the top of the head and chin sit between the guide lines.
4. Adjust **Brightness** if the photo is a little dark.
5. Press **Download photo** for a single photo, or **4 × 6 in print sheet** for printable copies.

## A worked example

You need a US passport photo. Ask someone to photograph you against a white wall, about 1.5 m away, at eye level. Choose **United States passport or visa: 2 × 2 in**, open the photo, zoom until your head fills the space between the lines, and center your face in the oval. Press **4 × 6 in print sheet**: you get six 2 × 2 in photos on one sheet, ready to print for about the cost of a standard photo print. A UK 35 × 45 mm sheet fits eight copies.

## Taking a good photo

- **Light from the front,** such as facing a window, to avoid shadows on the face and background.
- **Plain, light background** with nothing behind you.
- **Neutral expression,** mouth closed and eyes open, looking straight at the camera.
- **No hats, sunglasses or tinted glasses.** Many countries now ask you to remove glasses completely.
- **Hair away from your face** so your eyebrows and face outline are visible.
- **Use the back camera** held by someone else; selfies distort faces at arm's length.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing the wrong country size.** US photos are square; most others are 35 × 45 mm.
- **Head too small.** Zoom in until the top of the head and chin reach the guide lines.
- **Printing with "fit to page" turned on,** which shrinks the photos. Print at 100%.
- **Using an old photo.** Most countries need a photo taken within the last six months.

## Private by design

Your photo is processed in your browser and never uploaded, which matters for an image used on identity documents.
