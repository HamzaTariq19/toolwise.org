## When to use it

Black and White Image converts colour pictures to grayscale, pure black and white, or sepia. Use grayscale for timeless portraits and moody street photos, black and white for clean signatures, stamps and scanned documents that print sharply, and sepia for a vintage, warm look on family photos or invitations.

## How to make an image black and white

1. Tap **Choose image**, or drag a JPG, PNG, WebP or HEIC file onto the box.
2. Pick a **Style**: **Grayscale**, **Black & white** or **Sepia**.
3. For Grayscale and Sepia, adjust **Contrast** to make the image punchier or softer.
4. For Black & white, adjust the **Threshold**. Lower makes more of the picture white, higher makes more of it black.
5. Choose **Save as** and press **Download image**.

## A worked example

You need a clean signature image for documents, but your phone photo of a signature on paper has a grey background and a slight shadow. Open it, choose **Black & white**, and start at threshold 128. The paper turns white but a few faint strokes break up, so raise the threshold to 150 until the strokes are solid. Save as **PNG**: the result is crisp black ink on pure white, perfect for Sign PDF or a document.

## Tips for great black-and-white photos

- **Look for contrast and texture:** strong light, shadows, patterns and faces work well without colour.
- **Add a little contrast** to grayscale photos that look flat.
- **Use sepia sparingly;** it suits portraits and old-fashioned themes more than landscapes.
- **For documents,** good, even lighting matters more than the threshold value.

## Choosing the right style

- **Grayscale:** photos, portraits, artistic shots.
- **Black & white:** signatures, sketches, QR codes, scanned forms, stencils.
- **Sepia:** vintage looks, heritage projects, invitations.

## Printing tip

If you print documents in black and white anyway, converting signatures and stamps here first gives you a preview of how they'll print, and avoids grey, blotchy results from colour scans.

## Common mistakes

- **Using Black & white on photos,** which loses all the mid-tones and looks harsh.
- **A threshold that's too low** for pencil or faint pen, making lines disappear.
- **Saving signatures as JPG,** which adds grey smudges around the ink; use PNG.
- **Overdoing contrast,** which loses detail in shadows and highlights.
