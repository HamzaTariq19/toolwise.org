## When to use it

Use this calculator when you have several debts and want a clear order to pay them off. List every card and loan, add any spare money you can put towards them each month, and the tool works out when each debt will be gone, the date you will be debt-free, and how much interest the plan saves compared with paying only the minimums.

## How to use it

1. Choose your **Currency**.
2. For each debt, enter a **Name**, the **Balance**, the **APR %** and the **Minimum / month**. Use **Add debt** for more rows, or the remove button to delete one.
3. Enter the **Extra you can pay each month**. This is money on top of all the minimum payments.
4. Choose a **Method**: **Snowball (smallest balance first)** or **Avalanche (highest rate first)**.
5. Read **Debt-free in**, **Total interest**, **Interest saved** and the **Payoff order**, which shows the month each debt is cleared.

Below the results, the tool tells you how long paying only the minimums would take, and how the other method compares.

## Worked example

You have three debts:

- Credit card: $4,500 at 22.9%, minimum $135
- Car loan: $9,800 at 6.5%, minimum $290
- Store card: $850 at 27%, minimum $35

With $200 extra each month and the snowball method, the store card is gone in month 4, the credit card in month 18 and the car loan in month 26. You are debt-free in 2 years 2 months, pay $1,853 in interest and save $2,086 compared with paying only the minimums, which would take 3 years 6 months.

In this case the avalanche method costs the same, because the smallest debt also has the highest rate. With other debts, the gap can be hundreds of dollars.

## Tips

- **Keep paying the same total.** The plan only works if you keep the full monthly amount going after each debt is cleared.
- **Find the extra first.** Look for a fixed amount you can manage every month, even $50. Being steady matters more than the size.
- **Stop new borrowing.** A plan cannot keep up with new spending on the same cards.
- **Update the numbers every few months.** Enter the new balances to check you are on track.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving out small debts.** A forgotten store card or overdraft can quietly grow.
- **Using the wrong minimum.** Card minimums change as the balance falls. Use the current figure from your statement.
- **Choosing only by the numbers.** If quick wins keep you motivated, snowball can be the better plan even if it costs a little more.
