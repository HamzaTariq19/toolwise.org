## When to use it

Use this calculator to get a personal daily water target instead of a one-size-fits-all rule. It is helpful when you start a new exercise routine, travel somewhere hot, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or simply want a number to aim for with a water bottle.

## How to use it

1. Choose your **Units**: **kg and liters** or **lb and ounces**.
2. Enter your **Body weight**.
3. Enter your **Exercise per day** in minutes.
4. Choose your **Climate**: **Mild or cool** or **Hot or humid**.
5. Tick **Pregnant or breastfeeding** if it applies.
6. Read the amount under **Aim to drink about**, shown in liters or ounces and as a number of glasses.

## How it works

The tool starts with about 33 ml of water for every kilogram of body weight. It adds about 350 ml for every 30 minutes of exercise, 500 ml for a hot or humid climate, and 700 ml if you are pregnant or breastfeeding. Food provides roughly another 20% of the water you need, so this target covers drinks only.

## Worked example

You weigh 70 kg and walk for 30 minutes a day in a mild climate. The tool suggests about 2.7 liters a day, roughly 11 glasses of 250 ml, or 90 fl oz.

On a hot summer day, choose **Hot or humid** and the target goes up by about half a liter. If you add a one-hour run, enter 90 minutes of exercise in total and the target rises by another 700 ml. Using US units instead, a 154 lb person gets the same advice in fluid ounces.

## Tips

- **Spread it out.** Drink a glass with each meal and one between meals rather than a lot at once.
- **Use a marked bottle.** Knowing your bottle holds 750 ml makes it easy to count: about four refills for 2.7 liters.
- **Drink before you exercise.** Have a glass 30 minutes before a workout and sip during long sessions.
- **Eat water-rich foods.** Fruit, vegetables and soups help you reach your total.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until you are very thirsty.** By then you may already be a little dehydrated, especially in the heat.
- **Forgetting to adjust for exercise.** Heavy sweating can lose a liter or more an hour.
- **Treating the target as a strict rule.** It is a guide. Listen to your body and drink more when you are hot, ill or active.

This is a general guide, not medical advice.
